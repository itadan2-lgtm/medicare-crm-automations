Daily Workflow Guide

How to Use Your CRM

The Setup Guide got the autopilot running. This shows how to actually work your book of business day to day. Skim it once — it becomes second nature fast.

The idea

You enter the facts. The autopilot watches the rules.

You do this
Add clients, appointments, SOAs, and policies as they happen — then read the morning digest and clear anything flagged.
The autopilot does this
Checks SOA timing, enrollment windows, chargeback risk, and T65 birthdays every morning — then flags problems, files follow-up tasks, and messages you.
Every morning

Two minutes, start of day

  1. Read the digest that arrives by email or text — it says how many items were checked and how many need you.
  2. Open the Dashboard and glance at the This week section.
  3. Handle anything flagged using the workflows below.
The everyday workflows

The four things you'll do most

1  ·  📥 A new lead comes in
  1. If you use the web form, the lead lands in Leads (Intake) by itself, marked New. Otherwise, add a row yourself.
  2. Move its Status along as you work it: New → Contacted → Qualified.
  3. When they sign on, add them to Clients with Status = Client. That master record is what everything else links back to.
2  ·  🗓️ Book an appointment — and stay compliant the important one
  1. Add a row in Appointments: pick the Client, set Date/Time, Type, and Product Discussed.
  2. Create the matching SOA Records row: fill Date Signed, Format, and Products Covered, and link it to both the Client and this Appointment.
  3. Leave Compliance Status alone — the autopilot fills it. If the SOA is missing, signed too late, or doesn't cover the product, it flags the appointment and creates a fix-it task.
Rule of thumb: get the SOA on file before the appointment. That one habit keeps almost every compliance flag green.
3  ·  📜 Record a policy you wrote
  1. Add a row in Policies / Commissions: Client, Carrier, Plan Type, Effective Date, Commission Amount.
  2. Fill Chargeback Period (Days) from your carrier contract — the Carrier Reference table is your cheat-sheet for typical values.
  3. Leave Chargeback Risk Status alone — the autopilot watches the clock and sets it for you.
4  ·  🎂 Prospecting clients turning 65 (T65)

Nothing to do but work the tasks. The autopilot scans every client's DOB, tags anyone about six months from turning 65, and drops a follow-up Task in your list — so you reach them before another agent does.

Where you'll live

Dashboard & Tasks

📊 The Dashboard is your home base. The stat cards summarize your whole book — client mix, compliance, chargeback risk, lead pipeline — and This week surfaces exactly what needs attention now, so you rarely have to dig into the raw databases.

✅ Tasks / Follow-ups holds both the tasks you add yourself and the ones the autopilot files (SOA fixes, T65 outreach). Work them top-down by Due Date and Priority.

A few good habits
  • Log the SOA before or at the appointment — not after.
  • Fill in Chargeback Period (Days) on every policy, or chargeback tracking has nothing to watch.
  • Don't hand-edit the auto fields — Compliance Status, Election Period Tag, and Chargeback Risk Status are owned by the autopilot, and it will overwrite your changes on the next run.
  • This is a workflow aid, not legal advice — always verify SOA timing against current CMS guidance.
